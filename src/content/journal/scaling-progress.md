"On a scale of 0 to 10, where 0 is the worst this has ever been and 10 is the day after the miracle — where are you right now?"

It's a simple tool, but it does a few things at once. It turns something fuzzy into a number you can compare week to week, so progress (or a setback) is visible instead of just a feeling. And the more useful follow-up question is usually: "What's already making it a 4 and not a 2?" — which points straight at what's working, rather than only what isn't.

Over several sessions, the number itself matters less than the conversation it opens up about what moved it, even slightly, in either direction.
