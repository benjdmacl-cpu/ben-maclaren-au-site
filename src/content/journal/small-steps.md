Once there's some clarity on what "better" looks like and what's already working, the next question is always the same: what's one small, doable thing between now and next time?

Not the whole solution — just one step. It's tempting to aim for something bigger, especially when the problem feels big, but a step that's genuinely small enough to actually happen beats an ambitious one that doesn't.

Small steps compound. Each one gives you real information about what helps, which makes the next step easier to pick — and that's usually a faster way to real change than trying to solve everything in one move.
